Executive Summary

Fjord Analytics spends ≈ $142k / month (~$1.7M / year) on AWS. The estate is healthy and well-run in places, but the review found 17 savings opportunities worth ≈ $38k / month — about $457k / year, or 27% of the bill. The encouraging part for budget-holders: the biggest, safest savings need no code change — buying the commitments the steady-state workload already justifies, turning non-production off overnight, and clearing orphaned resources. The theme is not "over-built" — it is "under-committed and unmetered."

Spend Landscape — Where the Money Goes

Fjord's $142k / month is compute- and data-heavy, as you'd expect for an analytics SaaS: EC2, RDS and the analytics stack (OpenSearch + Redshift) together are ~half the bill. Two things stand out from the actuals: almost two-thirds of compute runs on-demand (little commitment coverage), and ~40% of spend carries no cost-allocation tag, so it can't be attributed to a team or product.

By account / environment · CUR

Under-committed

Only ~35% of eligible compute is on a Savings Plan or RI; the rest pays full on-demand. The single largest saving in the report is simply buying what the baseline already justifies.

Non-prod never sleeps

Dev, sandbox and staging total ~$23k/mo and run 24/7. A working-hours schedule (12h × 5d) reclaims roughly 65% of their idle time.

Unmetered spend

With ~40% untagged, no team sees its own bill. Allocation is the enabler that turns one-off savings into an ongoing culture.

Fjord's biggest cost problem isn't a runaway service — it's full-price rates on a predictable workload, and a bill nobody owns line-by-line.
Reading the landscape. Two structural facts set up the whole report. First, production dominates ($86k of $142k) and is genuinely busy — the savings there are rate (commit to it) and rightsizing (it's over-provisioned), not deletion. Second, the $23k/mo of non-production and the $13k/mo of data-transfer + NAT are where the cheap, safe wins live. The five levers that follow are ordered by savings-per-effort, so the fastest money is nearest the front.

Method & integrity statement
