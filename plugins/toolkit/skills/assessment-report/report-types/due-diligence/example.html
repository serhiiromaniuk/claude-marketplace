The technology works and the economics are attractive, but diligence surfaced 24 findings — two critical, five high — concentrated in a scaling-ceiling monolith, a fragile revenue-critical billing module, and organizational key-person risk. Company & Technology Overview

Loomly Labs sells a B2B workflow-automation platform — a visual builder plus a run-time engine that executes multi-step automations against ~40 third-party integrations. The product is a single Ruby-on-Rails monolith (Rails 6.1) with a React front-end, backed by one shared PostgreSQL using a shared-schema multi-tenancy model. It runs on AWS in a single region (us-east-1). 620 customers, 40 FTE, $8.4M ARR.

Single Rails deploy unit against one shared Postgres in one region. Green = healthy; red = the diligence findings. The revenue-critical billing module and synchronous heavy jobs sit inside the same monolith and share the same database.
Team shape · 40 FTE

Eng 22 (incl. 1 lead architect, 4 senior, 12 mid/junior, 3 QA-light, 2 SRE) · Product 5 · Design 3 · GTM 8 · G&A 2. One architect owns the engine + deploy pipeline.

Key-person concentration

Bus-factor 1 on the workflow engine, billing internals and the release process. Departure risk is the single largest organizational exposure to the thesis.

Thesis fit

The moat (620 customers, 112% NRR, deep integrations) is real; the constraint is that the current architecture caps growth near Meridian's target scale without re-platforming spend.

Loomly's problem is not that the technology is bad — it is that the parts that make money and the parts that carry the most risk live in the same box, owned by one person.
