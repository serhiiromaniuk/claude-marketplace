The Acme Widgets platform spans three independent infrastructure tenants. The good news for budget-holders: the backlog is small, mostly low-cost configuration work, and reversible, and the extra monthly run-cost to fix it is under $60. The theme is not "broken" — it is "unowned and unhardened."

Architecture — Three Tenants, One Game

Acme Widgets is split-brain by design history: development runs on rented bare-metal, production runs on AWS — in a different region than the accounts are configured for. The unusual part is the deploy path: CI on the bare-metal hosts builds and pushes straight into the AWS production account, while the AWS "non-prod" account holds only DNS and Terraform state.

CI on bare-metal builds & deploys into the AWS prod account. Non-prod AWS holds only DNS + Terraform state. Region split: config says eu-north-1; production compute lives in eu-central-1.
Split-brain topology

Dev/staging live on bare-metal; prod lives on AWS. The two halves share no platform, so a change proven in "non-prod" was never proven on prod-like infra.

No staging buffer

Because AWS non-prod has no compute, production is effectively the first AWS environment any new infrastructure ever touches.

Region drift

Profiles point at eu-north-1 but everything runs in eu-central-1 — a standing trap for operators reading config at face value.

The fastest path from a leaked credential to a production outage is shorter than it should be — and the architecture, not any single host, is why.

Cost Actuals & Backup / DR Posture

Production spend · AWS Cost Explorer, verified

Prod runs at ≈ $220 / month (~$2.6k/yr), trending down from $245 (Mar) → $220 (May). Non-prod is ≈ $1/mo (Route 53 only).

Read: spend is modest and healthy. The biggest lines are fixed infra (NAT, MQ, ELB) not compute — so the single-task prod is cheap, and adding HA (2nd task + Multi-AZ RDS) adds only ~$30–60/mo. Cost is not a blocker to fixing resilience.

  • Backups exist and are current — RDS daily snapshot taken today, 7-day retention + ~5-min PITR; Redis 7-day snapshots ✓
  • Gap is recovery speed, not data loss: no Multi-AZ standby → failure means a manual restore, not an automatic failover
  • No cross-region DR — a region outage = extended downtime
  • skip_final_snapshot=true in Terraform — a teardown leaves no safety snapshot (mitigated today by delete-protection)
  • A restore has never been tested
DR bottom line. Data durability is in good shape; availability is not. The same HA work in C3 (Multi-AZ RDS, Redis failover, a 2nd task) is what converts "manual restore over hours" into "automatic failover in minutes." A one-off restore drill (≈0.25 day) would validate the RTO numbers above — currently they are estimates, not proven.
Acme Widgets can recover its data — what it cannot yet do is stay up while it does. Durability is solved; availability is the open line item.

Impact & Business Case

The remediation is small in hours but the avoided losses are not symmetric: high-severity items defend against low-probability / high-cost events (a breach, a prod outage) plus one high-frequency / low-cost drain (wasted CI debugging). Bottom line. Acme Widgets's infrastructure is functional but unhardened and unowned. Three working days — front-loaded with sub-day security quick wins, for under $60/mo extra — moves the estate from "D / At-Risk" toward "B / Good", removes the two most dangerous exposures on day one, and stops a steady leak of developer time. The remaining open question is strategic: whether non-production should live on AWS to restore prod parity.
Three days of work and under sixty dollars a month stand between "D / At-Risk" and "B / Good." The asymmetry — not the effort — is the headline.
